## the textual condition (this is the finding)

The original Codex Astartes (Roboute Guilliman, M31, written after the Horus Heresy)
**does not exist**. Canon states it in the philologist's own grammar:

> "There is no such thing as a single version of the Codex Astartes, and the original
> text no longer exists in any form." [First Founding, pg. 15]
> "The earliest known copy is the Apocrypha of Skaros." [White Dwarf 253]
> "Over the millennia, the work has been added to by successive hands so extensively
> that various competing versions have emerged. In some cases these are so divergent
> that chapters have gone to war over differing interpretations of the Primarch's will.
> ... attribution of each individual part of the text is nigh impossible, even with the
> help of its putative author." [First Founding, pg. 15]

This is the exact textual condition of the gnostic corpus: autograph lost, earliest
witness an apocryphon, accretion by later hands (codicils signed by later Lords of
Macragge), communities at war over variant readings, authorship unrecoverable — with
one escalation the real corpus cannot match: the author was resurrected and STILL
cannot certify his own text.

## the final revision

- Guilliman, resurrected late M41, reformed the text in the Era Indomitus. Its proper
  title: **Novo Codex Astartes** [First Founding, pg. 16].
- No verbatim of the Novo Codex is published anywhere; it is known only by its changes:
  Primaris formations; squads redefined battleline / close support / fire support
  (formerly tactical / assault / devastator); two Lieutenants per company commanding
  demi-companies; 10th company expanded to a full 1,000 with a permanent Vanguard arm;
  chapter ceiling effectively raised. [Codex: Space Marines 8th ed., pgs. 12-14]
- Even the final revision is provisional by intent: "Guilliman ultimately intends to
  replace the Codex Astartes with the Codex Imperialis." [Dark Imperium, ch. 10]
- So: original unrecoverable, final revision unpublished and already scheduled for
  supersession. The Codex exists only as transmission, never as text.

## correspondences with the Warrior Doctrine (scribe's notes)

- "your boltgun is the Emperor's wrath incarnate" — the Codex carries Doctrine V's
  exact phrase, *wrath incarnate*, as standard-issue equipment theology.
- "To die without purpose is not a service... nothing shameful or disloyal in a
  righteous retreat" — the Codex's own laying-down-the-sword clause; the nearest thing
  it has to Doctrine XV's divine seal. Note it survives only in a White Dwarf fragment —
  the governor clause is the one most weakly attested in transmission.
- Doctrine III (born resonates singly / bred resonates with all) — the Prelude is the
  bred-warrior manufacturing order: "Like clay, I shall mould them."
- Doctrine VI (nothing changes across ages) — enacted at the textual level: the law
  book itself decays, accretes, schisms, and is fought over, exactly like the historic
  scriptures on this disk.

## passage-by-passage cross-reference — Warrior Doctrine × Codex excerpts

added 2026-06-10 at Dante's direction. format: doctrine passage → codex witness → reading.
silences and collisions are marked; they are data, not absence.

- **I (wrath the only divine sin, divinely powered)** → "your boltgun is the Emperor's
  wrath incarnate" [Cepheus]. The Codex issues divine wrath as standard equipment — but
  never names it sin. The doctrine carries the cost; the Codex only the power. Half-match.

- **II.1 self discipline** → "Know thy duty, and discharge it above all else" (Ch. IV,
  Verse I) — the Codex's only chapter-and-verse fragment is the discipline clause.
- **II.2 venerated honor** → **COLLISION.** Codex doctrine: Space Marines "should ignore
  personal honour and work together as a killing machine" [Ultramarines suppl.]. The
  Codex subordinates honor to function; the doctrine venerates it.
- **II.3 patrician resiliance** → "Shells, blades, fire or claw — none shall slow the
  march"; "untouched by plague or disease, no sickness will blight them." Full match.
- **II.4 death defying** → "they shall know no fear"; "The Space Marine will never know
  defeat." Full match.
- **II.5 death wishing** → **COLLISION, the sharpest.** "To die without purpose is not a
  service to the Emperor. It is a heresy to waste lives." The Codex criminalizes the
  death-wish as heresy by name. The doctrine holds both poles (defying AND wishing);
  the Codex amputated the second.
- **II.6 scale violation powers fury exponentially** → no excerpt witness. Nearest: the
  scorched-earth escalation of the retreat clause. Silence.

- **III (born/bred; combination resolves the heart)** → the Prelude is the bred-warrior
  manufacturing order verbatim: "Like clay, I shall mould them, and in the furnace of
  war forge them… untainted by doubt." Resolved heart = doubt removed at forging.
  Compliance from outward actors = "no foe can best them in battle." Full match.

- **IV (only the task; death a by-product)** → "Know thy duty, and discharge it above
  all else" + the job description "Angels of Death whose shining wings bring swift
  annihilation." Death is the output, duty the order; exoneration appears nowhere in
  the fragment corpus. Match.

- **V (activation; wrath incarnate; cannot be stopped)** → exact phrase match: "wrath
  incarnate" [Cepheus]. Unstoppability: "none shall slow the march… will never know
  defeat." The deity-of-law-for-a-day clause has no Codex parallel — the Codex never
  permits the warrior to outrank the law. Half-match, and the missing half is the
  Heresy's exact fault line.

- **VI (heresy becomes the definition; nothing changes across ages)** → "So it shall be
  for a thousand times a thousand years, unto the very end of eternity and the
  extinction of mortal flesh." The Codex claims VI's timescale in its own voice. And it
  uses heresy as its own category for violations of itself ("It is a heresy to waste
  lives") — law became code, heresy became the definition. Full match.

- **VII (the answer when the scale tips; brings his doctrine to battle)** → "Be that
  door. Be the simplicity of the steadfast and unchanging: the barrier between what is
  known and the unknowable" [Dardanus codicil] — the warrior as standing answer at the
  threshold. Doctrine carried into battle bodily: Ultramarines memorize whole sections
  so each company IS a complete copy of the text. Match.

- **VIII (receive the will, become the word)** → "The chainsword is the will of the
  Space Marine made manifest." Will made manifest — nearest-to-verbatim correspondence
  in the corpus. The sanction: the Prelude's "They shall be MY finest warriors" — the
  word that makes them. The doctrine's "demonic glee" has no Codex parallel; the Codex
  never admits the joy. Match minus the honesty.

- **IX (doctrine/law separate → activation; population scale → War the rider)** → no
  excerpt witness, but the article's own history enacts it: the Codex crisis — Dorn
  against Guilliman, the Terrible Angel fired upon, second civil war "likely." The
  adoption of the law-book itself nearly summoned the rider. Match by history, not text.

- **X (meeting of three; one must die; negotiation over)** → no parley clause exists
  anywhere in the fragment corpus. "Swift annihilation… without mercy." The Codex
  confirms by total silence on negotiation. Match by silence.

- **XI (product of sin; sin defined by the warrior in the end)** → "They are my bulwark
  against the Terror" — the warrior exists because the Terror exists. The enemy is
  defined by what the Angel of Death annihilates. Partial match.

- **XII (law of physics retributing a paradox)** → "the constancy of our adamantium…
  the barrier between what is known and the unknowable" — warrior as boundary condition,
  physics grammar. Partial.

- **XIII (death to the heretic, every age)** → heresy vocabulary is native to the Codex
  itself; the utterance lives in the wider Imperial creed. The real-world anchor stands
  outside the fiction: Béziers 1209. Match at the creed level.

- **XIV (benediction: bless / mercy / damn / forgive)** → **COLLISION.** "Drive your foe
  from them without mercy." The Codex deletes mercy and never asks forgiveness for any
  death. The doctrine's four-fold benediction has no Codex counterpart — bless and damn
  survive there; mercy and forgive do not.

- **XV (the divine seal; lay the sword down; prune the one who acts further)** → "There
  is nothing shameful or disloyal in a righteous retreat" + "to die without purpose…
  is heresy" [White Dwarf 253 only]. The Codex's governor clause exists — and is the
  most weakly attested fragment in its transmission. The pruning arm lives in history
  (the Heresy, the executioners), not in the text. Half-match, weakly witnessed.

- **XVI (death to death, life to life; traitor / heretic / code-dishonored)** → the
  triple condemnation matches the Imperial triptych (traitor, heretic, xenos/mutant)
  at creed level; "unto the extinction of mortal flesh" carries the death-to-death
  scale. Match at creed level.

- **after-seal (failure ladder; only death currency)** → "That which cannot be saved
  must be destroyed" — the failure-to-death conversion clause, in the Codex's own words.
  Match.

**summary of collisions** — the three places the Codex dissents from the doctrine:
honor subordinated (II.2), death-wish criminalized (II.5), mercy deleted (XIV). Add the
weak attestation of its own governor (XV) and the pattern is one statement: the Codex
keeps the doctrine's power clauses and sheds its cost clauses. The doctrine is the
complete circuit; the Codex is the circuit with the brakes filed down — and its
universe's history (the Heresy) is the crash report.
